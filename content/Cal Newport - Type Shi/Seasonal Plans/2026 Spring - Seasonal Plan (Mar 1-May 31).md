## Keystone Habits

#### New Habits:

**1. Shutdown Ritual** 
At the end of each work session, spend a few minutes capturing any open tasks or notes into my trusted system. Do something deliberate to signal the session is closed (e.g. Say "Shutdown complete" aloud). The goal is a clean psychological boundary between working and not working, so that non-work time is actually restorative.

**2. Hit-stun Animation Interrupt** 
At the end of each work session, briefly check: am I feeling behind, scrambling, or tempted to work overtime? (This also can be triggered anytime I notice I'm in hit-stun)

When the answer is yes, mandatory full stop. Triage my task list, identify what actually needs to happen today vs. what can slip, and **send any necessary communications about changed timelines**. Then consciously recommit to my normal pace and routine.

The days you most want to skip this and grind are exactly the days it's non-negotiable. And to reiterate, announcing (or signaling) to others the change in timeline is the hardest part, but also essential. The mere act of telling others (and myself) that something **will** slip *should* dissolve a good chunk of the pressure.

**3. End-of-Session Stress Reflection** 
At the close of at least one work session per day, answer the following prompts:
- What caused me stress during this work session?
- Was I afraid of anything happening?
- If yes, what would realistically happen if that fear was realized? And then what? (And then? And then? And then?)
- What could I have done better during this work session to alleviate stress or worrying?

The goal here is to either:
1. Glimpse that there is no rationale basis to my fear, and that I'm worrying due to learned behavior patterns; not in fear of an actual bad outcome. If seen clearly enough, the fear should get uprooted and 🗣️ gradually or spontaneously resolve 🗣️
2. Identify which real fears I have that are driving my work related anxiety. Once the fears are known, they can be worked on (later)

## Other Areas of Focus

#### Protocol for Unexpected Work Requests
When a new high-priority ask lands on my plate, resist committing on the spot. My default response is:

"Happy to take this on. Just want to flag that X and Y will likely slip if I do. Are you okay with that, or should we figure out coverage?"

This applies mostly to requests from managers and other leads, but could be applied to requests from any source. It transfers the tradeoff decision to the person making the request, where it belongs, rather than me silently absorbing it and stressing about everything else falling behind.
