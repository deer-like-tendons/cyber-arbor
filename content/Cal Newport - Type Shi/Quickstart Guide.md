# Multi-Scale Planning:

The purpose of multi-scale planning is to connect your long term goals w/ your daily schedule. By starting w/ the biggest picture (description of your ideal life) and letting that inform your quarterly plan, which informs your weekly plan, which informs your daily plan, you can focus on the immediately needful, whilst ensuring it's furthering your long term objectives.
### Annual:
- Describe the life you want using non-concrete first person declarative statements:
	- Reference the following buckets for inspiration:
		- Constitution: Body/Mind/Overall Wellbeing
		- Craft: Work, Mastery, Quality Leisure
		- Community: Friends, Family, Service, Others
		- Contemplation: Faith, Wonder, Curiosity
		- Celebration: Fun, Gratitude, Appreciation
	- Example of a good statement: "I'm paid to do intellectually stimulating work that I enjoy, and I work roughly as much as I'd prefer to."
	- Example of a bad statement: "My income exceeds 250k per year"

Annual desired life descriptions found in this folder: [[2026 - Desired Life Description]]

### Quarterly:
- Decide how to get closer to the desired vision given the tools and skills you have today (don't jump to make a huge life/career change and start from scratch; that's often a mistake)
- Don't spread yourself thin; Take big action in the buckets that need it the most.
	- Optionally use the [[Deep Life Stack 2.0 (Summary of Cal Newport Video)]] as a rough guide for the order of things to address
	- Decide on some "Keystone Habits" that you'll track every day that will result in you being materially closer to your desired life.

### Weekly 
**Planning** (Before or at the beginning of the week): 
- **Identify your 2-3 key priorities for the week** (not everything, just what actually matters most)
- **Schedule as much as you can; at the very least your deep work blocks** 
- **Scan for obstacles** (any days that will be particularly busy or fragmented or otherwise cause me to miss my goals? If so, come up w/ a plan to mitigate, or just let it slide; no biggie)

**Review** (After or near the end of the week):
- Did you maintain your keystone habits? (simple tracking)
- What went well? What interfered?
- Any adjustments needed for next week?

**Weekly Template**:
This is a template of what your ideal week looks like. The purpose is to simplify the decision making around planning your day, and to give clarity on which days/times you should be protecting from fragmentation.

Additionally, weekly templates can offer plans to handle unavoidable disruptions. For example, I get infusions every 8 weeks on Tuesdays. I could add to my weekly template a specific structure to accommodate these days.

### Daily:
Prioritize the tasks that move you closer to accomplishing your weekly goal, and don't be afraid to take **real, decisive action** to remove things that distract.

(Optionally) Utilize time-blocking to allocate each minute of the work day to some task. If plans change or something comes up, adjust your schedule accordingly. Some is better than none. Time-blocking portions of the day is better than a totally unstructured day.


# Misc Tools To Incorporate:

### Shutdown Ritual:
Spend time at the end of each workday to square away your work. All notes and todo's should be input to a trusted system; nothing should need to be kept in the mind.

After this is complete, do something specific that signifies to yourself that you've completed your shutdown ritual, and therefore do not need to think about work. A simple example is verbally saying "Shutdown Complete". It's somewhat important to actually *do* something to complete the ritual, as the memory of doing that thing can help permit total disconnection from work

### Normalizing Unavailability:
Regularly block-off time on your calendar during which you are unavailable*. You should pick these times as far in advanced as you can. This works to prevent others from scheduling meetings during your prime productive hours, and lightly trains the muscle of setting reasonable boundaries in a work environment.

\* - Being totally unavailable during work hours might not fly with some managers. A good workaround to this is to set a status like "DnD - Deep Work Block Until HH:MM - Call For Urgent Matters - 555-555-1234". This way, you're technically available, but literally no one will ever call you.


## Deep Work:
Deep work entails working with intention and without interruption for an extended period of time (usually >45 minutes). Blocks of deep work are super goated. You get more done in less time, and they're much more enjoyable and rewarding than the shallow, distracted, fragmented work we're all used to.

To facilitate deep work, intentionality is essential; deep work can happen by accident when we get swept up in a flow state, but it's rare thanks to our distraction filled work environments. 

**Remove Distractions:**
- Physically lock yourself away from others.
- Get out of eye-shot from anything that could draw your attention (certain windows need not be overlooked)
- Block out sounds. Headphones playing music, or even just brown noise prevent you from hearing anything in your environment that could require intervention
- Your phone should be nowhere in arms reach
- You should not be contactable aside from emergencies (see [[#Normalizing Unavailability]])

**Utilize Deep Work Rituals:**
Having a ritual can help you lock in. Here's mine:
1. Close my office door
2. Put on my headphones and play music or brown noise
3. Block calendar (if not blocked already)
4. Go on DnD mode on my work's messaging app
5. Set my status to "DnD - Deep Work Block Until HH:MM - Call For Urgent Matters - 555-555-1234"
6. Start timer (so I know when to get to stopping point)

If I do all these things, ain't no way I'm about to dilly dally or mess around.

**Deep Work Block Duration:**
You can go as long as you like, but you should prefer quality over duration. Start out by keeping your sessions on the shorter size, but keep your effort sharp. If you're checking email, getting distracted, or straight up not working near the end of your work sessions, they need to be shorter. Don't even let the possibility of not being laser focused during your deep work sessions creep into reality.
